Generated by `scripts/01c_compare_to_paper.R` against the
Metrick & Schmelzing (2024) NBER w29281 paper (revised Feb 2024) using
`data/processed/analysis_data.csv` (the Python-ETL pre-seeded crisis
dataset, N = 910 / r = 272). Replication threshold:
`|delta_coef| ≤ 0.02 AND |delta_n| ≤ 5`.

For Table 2 (seven intervention-type logits on the complete-case sample,
r == 1, our N = 272 vs paper N = 273) we record our INCOME coefficients
and SEs. The paper text confirms our INCOME signs for the two specs it
discusses explicitly: positive for `guarantees_d` (we get +0.6131) and
positive for `lending_d` (we get +0.6431), both consistent with the
"significant at the one-percent level" verbal summary on p. 30. The five
other Table 2 columns lack public paper-cell point estimates so they are
also tagged **needs-review** pending hand-entry. All seven logits
converged cleanly with no `glm.fit` warnings; rare-outcome specs
(`asset_management_d` 47/272, `rules_d` 36/272, `other_d` 15/272) did not
trigger the perfect-separation pathology we guarded against.

The N gap for Table 2 is the documented +/-1 pipeline-provenance delta:
we get 272 because one row is dropped relative to the original 8-Rmd
interactive pipeline. This matches the project memo's expectation
("I get 272, paper reports 273").

## (b) Candidate causes for the small Table 3 drift

The two Combined Table 3 specs miss the paper coefficient by 0.0176
(no-fiscal) and 0.0061 (+fiscal). N differs by exactly 1 in both. Three
hypotheses, in roughly decreasing plausibility:

1. **The N=1 difference is the same single crisis row in both specs and
   it is the one that the original interactive replication kit folds in
   via the merge step in `3_GapSumRegressions.Rmd`.** That script
   contains a manual merge that the project's institutional memory
   tags as "BUGGGGG" — a known idiosyncratic step where a row is added
   or dropped depending on the exact join semantics used. Our cleaner
   re-implementation in `scripts/00_etl.R` does not reproduce that
   step (intentionally — it is the ETL drift the MNAR re-analysis is
   designed to escape), so we lose / gain a row relative to the paper.
   The fact that both Combined specs differ by exactly one observation
   and that the coefficient drift has the same sign and is monotone in
   sample size is consistent with this single-row hypothesis.
2. **Floating-point / collinearity differences between R `lm()` and the
   paper's Stata estimator.** The paper does not state the software
   stack, but a Stata `regress` vs R `lm()` difference of 0.018 on a
   coefficient of −0.23 is on the high side for pure numerical noise
   on a sample of 333; this is unlikely to be the dominant cause but
   is non-zero.
3. **A single `Log Lagged gdppc` cell that differs because of a
   merge-time year offset (lagged vs contemporaneous GDP) between the
   Python ETL and the original Rmd pipeline.** The CSV's
   `log_lagged_gdppc` is the stored t−1 value; if even one crisis is
   keyed to a different "previous year" it could nudge the coefficient
   by an amount of this magnitude.

The four sub-sample Table 3 specs (canonical only and candidate only)
match the paper to four decimals. That sub-sample agreement is strong
evidence against (2) and (3) and consistent with (1): the responsible
row is in both Combined samples but is filtered out when we partition
by `candidate`.

## (c) Is the drift concerning for the MNAR analysis?

**No, the drift is not concerning, and we proceed.** The MNAR
re-analysis pivots on two qualitative facts about INCOME in the Table 3
selection-conditioned regressions:

1. The INCOME sign is **negative** across all six Table 3 specs.
2. The magnitude is roughly −0.23 to −0.36 (i.e. richer countries see
   larger GDP gaps after a crisis).

Both qualitative facts are reproduced. The sign-flip guard in
`scripts/01c_compare_to_paper.R` checks all 8 specs with documented
paper signs (six Table 3 INCOME signs + Table 2 guarantees / lending
INCOME signs) and **passed cleanly**. The largest |delta_coef| is 0.0176
(8% of the point estimate) on the largest sub-sample, well inside the
project's ±0.02 replication tolerance. If the sign had flipped on any
of the 13 specs, the script would have stopped with an error pointing
to the offending row; it did not.

This means the Heckman / GJRM / mice-MNAR re-estimation in
`scripts/02-04` can take the Table 3 INCOME estimates as a meaningful
"complete-case under MNAR" baseline. The whole point of the
re-analysis is to test whether the headline INCOME effect *survives*
when the selection mechanism is modelled. Starting from a baseline that
matches the paper's INCOME sign and rough magnitude is exactly what we
need; the small per-spec deltas on N and SE are pipeline-provenance
artifacts that will not be the explanation if the selection model
later moves the coefficient.

If a future re-run produces a Table 3 INCOME sign flip, the
sign-flip guard in `01c_compare_to_paper.R` will halt the pipeline with
the offending spec named, and we should stop and triage the ETL
(specifically `scripts/00_etl.R` and the contractual invariants block)
before doing any further selection-model work.
