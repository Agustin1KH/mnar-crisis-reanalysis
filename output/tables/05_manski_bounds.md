## Construction

Sample for bounds: post-1800 with `log_lagged_gdppc` observed (N = 644). Partition by INCOME quartile (Q1 = lowest, Q4 = highest); within each quartile, treat `r == 1` as 'observed by paper's complete-case logit' and `r == 0` as 'unavailable'. Per-quartile worst-case bounds on E[y = 1 | quartile]:

    LB = P(y=1 | quartile, r=1) * P(r=1 | quartile)            (missing all 0)
    UB = P(y=1 | quartile, r=1) * P(r=1 | quartile) + P(r=0)   (missing all 1)

Slope bounds (top minus bottom quartile difference in P(y=1)):

    slope_LB = LB(top) - UB(bottom)    (most adversarial)
    slope_UB = UB(top) - LB(bottom)    (most favourable)

INCOME quartile means: bottom = 7.374, top = 10.152 (deltaX = 2.778 log GDPpc points). The paper's logit coefficient is converted to an approximate probability difference between the top and bottom INCOME quartile means via the local linearisation:

    delta P(y=1) ~ beta * P_bar * (1 - P_bar) * deltaX

where `P_bar` is the complete-case base rate of `y=1`. This conversion is approximate; valid for moderate base rates and used solely to make the logit and Manski quantities comparable on the same scale.

## Reading

**Containment of point estimates within the no-assumption bounds.** Paper's logit point estimate (converted to prob-diff scale) lies inside the Manski bounds for 6 / 6 outcomes; MICE-MAR pooled estimate for 6 / 6; MICE-MNAR pooled for 6 / 6. The Manski bounds are necessarily wide because they treat the 514 'r == 0' cases per outcome as having unknown y under the worst possible assignment; the parametric estimators (paper's logit, MICE-MAR, MICE-MNAR) all impose stronger assumptions and produce point estimates that the bounds cannot rule out. Containment is the minimum sanity check; it confirms that no parametric estimator extrapolates outside the data's no-assumption range.

**Zero is inside the Manski bounds for ALL 6 outcomes.** Without parametric assumptions on the missingness mechanism, the data cannot reject 'no INCOME effect on the probability of any of these interventions' for any outcome. The paper's logit and the MICE estimates impose parametric assumptions to identify a sign and a magnitude; without those assumptions, the data alone is consistent with no INCOME effect on each binary outcome at the worst-case end of the bounds.
